Community Deception Techniques: Coordinated Social Engineering in WhatsApp Group Scams

IEEE CARS 2026 Presented in person · Grand Forks, North Dakota

This research examines how scammers use coordinated social engineering techniques within WhatsApp group environments to manipulate users and establish credibility. Rather than relying on a single suspicious message, these scams can involve multiple participants, coordinated interactions, and deceptive communication strategies designed to make fraudulent activity appear legitimate.

The project investigates these techniques to better understand how coordinated scam activity operates and how its behavioral patterns can inform cybersecurity awareness and detection efforts.

Research Process

The work combines academic research and analysis of social engineering techniques to better understand how coordinated scams operate.

01

Literature Review

Reviewed existing cybersecurity research on online scams, social engineering, messaging platforms, and emerging deception techniques to establish the background for the study.

02

Scam Behavior Analysis

Examined how coordinated social engineering can occur in WhatsApp group environments, focusing on deceptive communication, interactions between participants, and tactics used to influence potential victims.

03

Research and Documentation

Contributed to the research process by reviewing findings, organizing relevant information, and helping document the techniques and behaviors examined in the paper.

04

Conference Presentation

The research was presented at the 2026 IEEE Cyber Awareness and Research Symposium (CARS), providing an opportunity to share the work with researchers and cybersecurity professionals.

Ongoing Research & Development

Cryptocurrency Scam Website Detection

In Development

Building on research into online fraud and deceptive behavior, I am currently contributing to the development of a tool designed to investigate and identify potential cryptocurrency scam websites.

The goal is to develop a system that can examine characteristics of suspicious websites and connect relevant information, such as cryptocurrency wallets, transaction activity, and interactions associated with potentially fraudulent platforms. This work is ongoing, and the detection pipeline is still being developed and evaluated.

Current Technical Direction

Website Analysis

Exploring website characteristics and features that may help distinguish legitimate platforms from potential scam websites.

Cryptocurrency Investigation

Working toward incorporating wallet addresses, transaction information, and related blockchain activity into the analysis process.

Machine Learning

Developing a Python-based detection pipeline using LightGBM to evaluate extracted features and identify patterns associated with potential scam websites.

What We're Working Toward

The current work focuses on developing and evaluating the initial detection pipeline. As the research progresses, our team is also exploring additional tools and approaches that could expand the investigation process and provide a more complete picture of cryptocurrency-related scams.

This is an active research effort. The system's capabilities, analysis methods, and supporting tools are still being refined.
